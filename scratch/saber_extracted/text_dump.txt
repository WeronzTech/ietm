1.2 DescriptionThe SSS Defence Saber is the result of blending the latest advances in sniper systems design with feedback from the most demanding of military users. Applying state of the art design, manufacturing and materials, every component of the rifle has been engineered to be lighter and stronger than several of the world’s best sniper weapons in use. The weapon operates with the .338 Lapua Magnum cartridge, delivering outstanding sub-MOA accuracy for distances of over 1500m.

4.4. Magazine Magazine of sniper rifle is a centre-feeding box magazine. The magazine box has been made of steel plate. Upper part of the magazine box forms feeding lips that hold cartridges in the magazine as well as guide them during feeding. Upper front part of the magazine box has been formed so that the magazine follower is prevented from coming out of the magazine box. On the rear wall of the magazine box there is an opening that acts a counterpart to the magazine catch located in the trigger guard. On the rear bottom corners of the magazine sides there are riveted studs which help in removal of the magazine.Cartridge follower has been designed so that it supports cartridge during chambering. Notch on the front of the cartridge follower supports the last cartridge of the magazine during chambering. Notch also serves as a support when single cartridges are chambered through the ejection port. Inside the magazine box there is a covering plate, which guides also the magazine follower spring. Magazine catch and its spring have been fastened to the front of the trigger guard with a pin. When inserting a loaded magazine push it upright into the magazine well and press the magazine until the magazine catch locks it into its position. When detaching the magazine press the magazine catch by the thumb and pull the magazine out of the rifle with the aid of the detaching studs located at the lower rear corners of the magazine.

INSPECTION OF TOOLS AND GAUGES Before carrying out any maintenance, repairs or checks on any parts of the weapon, make sure that the tools and gauges to be used are clean, in proper working order and not damaged in any way. 3. TESTS, INSPECTIONS AND ADJUSTMENTS This section sets out the tests, inspections and adjustments to be performed on assemblies or individual components on the sniper rifle in order to ascertain their efficiency. 3.1 Inspecting the bore The barrel wear due to the excessive amount of shooting appears as the wear of the chamber throat / free bore. The diameter of the throat will increase and, at the same time, it will lengthen towards the muzzle. www.sssdefence.com31

--- PAGE 32 ---
The throat wear usually deteriorates the accuracy of the rifle and degreases the muzzle velocity. To inspect the throat for wear barrel wear gauge can be used. If the barrel wear gauge can be pushed from the chamber end of the barrel for more than 200 mm into the bore the barrel wear can be considered as remarkable and may due to somewhat lowered muzzle velocity. The final judgment of the barrel replacement should, however, always be based on the results of test shooting. 
3.2 Inspecting the headspace Head spacing is inspected with GO-/NO GO-gauges. With the GO-gauge inserted into the chamber the bolt must close smoothly, but with NO GO-gauge inserted into the chamber the bolt must not close at all. With NO GO-gauge inserted into the chamber, excessive force must be avoided while closing the bolt. This may result in deformation of locking surfaces, which in turns contributes to excessive wear of the headspace and the movements of the bolt become stiffer. If the bolt can be closed with the NO GO gauge inserted into the chamber , the rifle must not be used until the head spacing has been corrected by replacing the barrel or the bolt. 
Fig. 15 –Barrel sectional view

3.3 Inspecting the firing pin protrusion Guage For inspection of the firing pin protrusion the bolt must be removed from the receiver and decocked by turning the bolt shroud counter-clockwise, until the firing pin decocks. The firing pin protrusion can now be inspected using the appropriate gauge or by measuring it with a dial caliper. The firing pin protrusion should be 1.4 – 1.8 mm, when measured from the bolt face. If the test fails the firing pin assembly must be replaced. The firing pin is cocked by turning the bolt shroud clockwise with the cocking wrench, until the sear stops to its resting groove.

3.4 Inspecting the safety The functioning of the safety should be inspected periodically. 
With the safety switched ON practically no trigger movement after the first stage should be encountered. Do not, however, use excessive force when testing the safety. Excessive force may damage the safety mechanism and lead to an unreliable operation of it. If any undue movement of the trigger is recognized after the first pull the trigger mechanism must be replaced with a spare and the faulty unit must be delivered to a qualified maintenance personal for repair.
All inspections of the trigger mechanism and the safety must be carried out ONLY when the rifle is absolutely free of cartridges!
