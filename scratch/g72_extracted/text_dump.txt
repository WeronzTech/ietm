4. INSTRUCTIONS FOR USEThe SSS DEFENCE G-72 SMG follows an eight-step operation cycle. The first half is categorized by what is called the ‘recoil stroke’ starting with Firing and ending with Locking.FIRINGWhen the trigger is pulled, the firing pin strikes the cartridge primer, igniting the powder. This generates gas pressure, which propels the bullet down the barrel.Delay Mechanism (Rollers Engaged)The rollers located between the receiver and the blowback delay block remain engaged with the locking surfaces in the receiver, delaying the rearward movement of the carrier until safe amounts of pressure is maintained inside the barrel before the next step.UNLOCKING (Roller Disengagement)Breech mechanism is activated, which results in unlocking of the chamber. Chamber is where the cartridge sits.
www.sssdefence.com16
--- PAGE 17 ---
EXTRACTIONAs the carrier moves back, the spent casing is extracted from the chamber and ejected out of the ejection port by the ejector.COCKINGDuring the rearward movement of the carrier, the recoil spring is compressed and the firing mechanism is cocked. When the carrier reaches the end of its travel, the recoil spring pushes it forward again.FEEDINGThe forward movement of the carrier picks up a new round from the magazine and chambers it, preparing the weapon for the next shot.LOCKINGThe carrier moves forward, and the roller drops downwards, re-engaging with the locking surfaces to delay the next firing cycle.www.sssdefence.com17

The SSS DEFENCE G-72 features three fire control selector positions: SAFE, SEMI & AUTO. In the SAFE position, the firearm is prevented from firing by mechanically blocking the trigger’s movement. The selector can be maneuvered only when the hammer is cocked. The SEMI position enables semi-automatic firing, allowing one round to be fired with each trigger pull. AUTO then facilitates burst shots. The ambidextrous selector is designed for use by both right- and left-handed shooters, with each position clearly marked by pictographs for easy identification.

1.Prepare the Rifle•Ensure the firearm is unloaded and pointed safely. •Securely attach MBUS sights. •Choose a zeroing distance (e.g., 25m, 50m, or 100m). 2.Adjust Sights•Rear Sight: Adjust windage (1 Click = 0.5 MOA at 100 meters) with the knob (clockwise = right, counterclockwise = left). •Front Sight: Adjust elevation (1 Click = 1 MOA at 100 meters) using the tool (clockwise = lower impact, counterclockwise = raise impact). 3.Fire and Observe•Fire a 3-5 shot group at the target. •Check the point of impact relative to your aim. 4.Make Corrections•Adjust rear sight for windage and front sight for elevation. 5.Confirm Zero•Repeat steps until shots consistently align with the point of aim. 6.Finalize•Record settings for future reference.

Before using your new firearm for the first time, ensure the barrel is cleaned thoroughly. After firing, clean the barrel bore and cartridge chamber using a clean patch attached to a cleaning rod. Replace the patch as needed until it comes out clean. If the bore is heavily soiled or has carbon buildup, apply a suitable gun cleaning solution to the bore and chamber. Allow it to sit for at least 10 minutes (or follow the specific instructions provided in the cleaning solution’s manual). Afterward, use an appropriate brush to remove any remaining residue.

4.3 Cleaning the Barrel Bore and Cartridge Chamber

Use a clean patch to dry the bore and cartridge chamber, ensuring all residues are removed. Repeat the process if needed. To prevent muzzle damage, insert the cleaning rod and brush into the barrel through the chamber (Refer Figures M & N to disassemble weapon). Push the brush all the way through the barrel. Avoid reversing the direction while the brush is inside the barrel, as this could cause it to jam. Always use the appropriate, dedicated barrel bore cleaning brushes for the correct caliber.
Clean the other parts of the weapon, including the external surfaces of the receiver, using a dry cloth and a suitable brush or wooden scraper. You may use preservation oil or kerosene during the cleaning process. However, avoid using bore cleaning solutions, as they are highly corrosive and may remain in the joints between components, potentially causing severe corrosion. Ensure that no cloth remnants or brush bristles are left in any part of the gun.After cleaning, wipe the bore, cartridge chamber, and all accessible parts with a cloth lightly oiled or use oil spray. Remove any excess oil. Before firing, ensure the barrel’s bore is dry. If using the gun in very low temperatures, remove the oil from all accessible parts or use a special lubricant designed for such conditions, as standard lubricants may thicken and cause malfunctions in extreme cold.For long-term storage in harsh climates, apply preservative grease, but be sure to remove it completely before using the gun again. Always point the gun in a safe direction. Remove the magazine and check that there is no round in the chamber or magazine well.
